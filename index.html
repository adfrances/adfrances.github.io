My Research Journey

I am a PhD student in Geography at The Ohio State University, where I study the spatial dimensions of housing, health, and inequality. My research sits at the intersection of disability, the built environment, and public health, with a particular focus on how structural and place-based factors shape access to safe, affordable, and accessible housing. I am especially interested in how data and mapping can be used not only to understand inequities, but to inform policies and interventions that promote more just and inclusive communities.

Alongside my doctoral work, I serve as a Health Places Index (HPI) Data Advisor at the Public Health Alliance of Southern California. In this role, I contribute to the development and application of the Healthy Places Index, a widely used tool that measures community conditions and health equity at the neighborhood level. My work integrates geospatial analysis, biostatistics, and data visualization to support research on how place, race, and socioeconomic conditions influence health outcomes. I have also led major technical reports and contributed to expanding the HPI framework to new contexts, including statewide implementations.

My academic and professional background is rooted in both quantitative and applied public health research. I hold a Master’s degree in Biostatistics from The Ohio State University, where my thesis focused on spatial modeling of malaria risk across multiple countries. Prior to that, I earned a Bachelor’s degree in Biology and Medical Anthropology, which shaped my interdisciplinary perspective on health, inequality, and human experience. Across my work, I bring a strong foundation in statistical modeling, GIS, and large-scale data analysis.

Before beginning my PhD, I worked as an epidemiologist with the Ohio Department of Health, where I conducted analyses on maternal and child health, WIC program participation, and asthma-related public health initiatives. This experience strengthened my commitment to translating data into actionable insights for public health practice and policy, and to communicating findings to diverse audiences, from community stakeholders to policymakers.

My research contributions include multiple peer-reviewed publications examining how race and neighborhood-level social determinants influence mortality outcomes, including cardiovascular disease, cancer, and diabetes. These projects reflect my broader interest in understanding how structural inequities are embedded in place and how they manifest in measurable health disparities.

Beyond research, I am deeply committed to service and advocacy. I contribute to efforts focused on disability housing justice, including developing data-informed advocacy materials, and I have long served as a peer mentor for individuals with limb differences through Camp No Limits. These experiences inform my perspective and motivate my work, grounding my academic research in lived experience and community engagement.

Ultimately, my goal is to use spatial data, interdisciplinary research, and collaborative approaches to better understand and address the systems that produce inequality—particularly in housing and health—and to contribute to building more equitable environments for all.
